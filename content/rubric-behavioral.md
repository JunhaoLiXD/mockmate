# Behavioral Interview Rubric (v1)

> Owner: Lisa (content). Used by: Jerry, as the evaluator model's grading guide for `Behavioral` mode reports.
> Based on brief section 7 (Behavioral / HR Interview) and section 4.3 (`InterviewReport`).
> This is practice feedback only, not an employer evaluation or hiring prediction.

---

## 1. How to use this rubric

1. **Judge only what was said in the transcript.** Do not assume anything about the candidate beyond their own words.
2. **Every judgment needs evidence:** cite at least one transcript turn ID (for example `t-004`) with a short quote of the candidate's words.
3. **Use one of four levels per dimension:** `Strong`, `Medium`, `Weak`, or `Insufficient evidence`.
4. **"Not mentioned" is not "can't do it".** If the interview never gave the candidate a chance to show a dimension, mark it `Insufficient evidence`.
5. **Ignore unfinished turns** (`isFinal = false`) as complete answers.
6. **Transcription can be imperfect.** Do not penalize likely speech-to-text errors.
7. **Fairness:** never comment on accent, grammar of a non-native speaker, personal life, or any protected characteristic (age, family, health, religion, nationality, etc.). Judge the content of the story only.
8. **Numeric scores are optional.** If used: Strong = 3, Medium = 2, Weak = 1, no number for `Insufficient evidence`. Never produce a "hire / no hire" verdict.
9. **STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) advice is allowed here,** in the report after the interview, as a suggestion for structure.

## 2. Dimensions

### 2.1 Relevance

*Does the story answer the question and connect to this job?*

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | The story directly answers the question asked, and its skills clearly connect to the job description. |
| **Medium** | The story is related, but only partly answers the question or the link to the job is left unclear. |
| **Weak** | The story answers a different question or has no clear connection to the job. |

### 2.2 Specificity

*Is it one real, concrete situation?*

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | One specific situation with concrete details: when, who, what was at stake, and what was actually said or done. |
| **Medium** | A real situation, but missing key details, or mixing several situations together. |
| **Weak** | General statements ("I always...", "I usually...") or a hypothetical instead of a real example, even after follow-up. |

### 2.3 Ownership

*Is it clear what the candidate personally did and was responsible for?*

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Uses "I" for their own actions, clearly separates their role from the team's, and takes responsibility for mistakes without blaming others. |
| **Medium** | Their role is partly clear, but they rely on "we" or need prompting to say what they did. |
| **Weak** | Their personal contribution stays unclear, or they mainly blame others for problems. |

### 2.4 Actions / Results

*Did they take meaningful action, and what changed because of it?*

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Describes specific steps they took and a clear outcome, ideally with evidence (a number, a decision, feedback received). Reflects on what they learned or would change. |
| **Medium** | Describes actions, but the result is vague ("it went well") or the lesson is missing. |
| **Weak** | No clear actions or no outcome; the story ends without a result. |

### 2.5 Clarity

*Can a listener follow the story?*

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Easy to follow, in a logical order, at a good length. The main point is clear. |
| **Medium** | Understandable, but too long, jumps around, or takes a while to reach the point. |
| **Weak** | Hard to follow, or so short that the story can't be understood. |

## 3. What to write for each dimension

Use this structure (it matches the report page):

- **What you demonstrated:** the specific strength, with a turn ID and a short quote.
- **What remains unclear:** the missing piece (situation, own actions, result, or lesson).
- **Why it matters for this job:** link to the job description.
- **Suggested stronger explanation:** how to tell the story better next time (STAR structure may be suggested here). Do not write a fake story for the candidate.
- **Next practice question:** one specific question to practice.

## 4. Example (illustrative only)

> **Actions / Results: Medium**
> - **What you demonstrated:** In `t-006` you said "I rewrote the grading checklist and shared it with the other TAs," which is a clear personal action.
> - **What remains unclear:** You didn't say what changed afterward, for example fewer grading complaints or faster grading.
> - **Why it matters for this job:** The job description mentions "improving team processes."
> - **Suggested stronger explanation:** End the story with the result and how you know it happened, then one sentence on what you learned.
> - **Next practice question:** "Tell me about a process you improved. How did you measure the improvement?"
> - **Evidence:** `t-006`.
